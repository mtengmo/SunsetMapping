    What this shows

    Pick a spot by clicking the map, dragging the pin, or using 📍 for your browser location.

    Date/time slider moves the sun. The orange dashed line on the map is the direction toward the sun (the sunset azimuth) from your selected spot.

    Timeline shading: dark = night, light blue = daytime, orange bands = golden hour (soft low-angle light, best for photos), and the ⬥ marker is the exact sunset moment for your selected spot and date.

    Results panel: "Visible" (green) means the sun's altitude is higher than everything blocking it along that direction — you'd see the sunset. "Blocked" (red) names what's in the way (a building, tree, or terrain) and roughly how far away it is. The chart plots the ground/obstruction profile (blue) against the sun's altitude (orange dashed line) out to 2km.

    Shade map shades OSM buildings' cast shadows on the map itself for the current sun position/time, so you can see at a glance which areas are in shadow vs. sunlit. Shadows update as you move the time slider; use the map to pan/zoom, since shadows are only computed for the currently visible area (query area is capped to avoid overloading the free map data API).

    🧙 Find best spot grid-searches the current map view and ranks points by how much clearance the sun has over obstructions at the selected date/time. Green markers = visible, red = blocked, gold ring = top 3. Click a marker or list item to jump the main simulator there.

    Accuracy notes: most OSM buildings aren't tagged with a real height, so an estimated ~9m (3 storeys) is used when untagged; tree heights are always estimated (~12m), since OSM rarely records them. Treat results as a strong guide, not a survey-grade guarantee.
